# Travel Requests

A travel request is asked before a trip: where you are going, when, why, and roughly how much it will cost. Your manager, or the project's Project Manager when the trip is for a project, approves or rejects it before money is spent. It lives in the **Expenses** app, under **Travel Requests**.

A travel request is optional. You can record and submit an expense without one.

## Filing a request

Open **My Travel Requests** and choose **New**:

| Field | What to enter |
| --- | --- |
| **Purpose** | Why you are going (required) |
| **Destination** | Where you are going (required) |
| **Start Date** and **End Date** | The first and last day of the trip (required). **Days** counts both |
| **Estimated Amount** | One total for the whole trip, in your organization's currency, whoever pays for each part (required) |
| **Account** | The customer the trip is for, if any |
| **Project** | The project the trip is for, if any (with Project Delivery on). Closed and Cancelled projects are not offered |
| **Itinerary** | The plan in your own words |
| **Notes** | Anything else |

Attach files in the **Files** tab. **Source** says how the record arrived, and **Review Notes** is where an assistant or a rule writes what it found for the approver. Neither decides anything: an assistant can draft the request, estimate the amount or check it, working as you through the API, and the decision stays with the approver.

## Itinerary lines

A trip with several legs can list them one by one under **Itinerary Lines** on the request's page. Choose **New** for each leg:

| Field | What to enter |
| --- | --- |
| **Kind** | Flight, Train, Car, Hotel or Other (required) |
| **From** and **To** | Where the leg starts and where it goes; for a hotel, **To** is where it is (**To** required) |
| **Start Date** and **End Date** | The day of the leg (required), and for a hotel the day you check out |
| **Details** | A seat, a hotel you prefer, other wishes |
| **Booking Reference** | The confirmation, once it is booked |

The legs are listed in the order of the trip. A leg has no amount: the request's **Estimated Amount** is the one total. Lines are optional, and a request with none is complete.

## Submitting and approving

| Status | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| **Draft** | You are still filling it in |
| **Submitted** | Sent to its approver, and locked while it waits |
| **Approved** | Yes. The plan no longer changes |
| **Rejected** | Sent back. Move it to Draft, change the plan and submit again |
| **Cancelled** | The trip was called off after approval. Final |
| **Closed** | The trip is over. Final |

Choose **Submit** to send it. Who decides depends on the **Project**:

- **On a project**: the project's **Project Manager**, who also approves the project's expenses, so the trip and what it costs go to the same person. When the project has no Project Manager, or the trip is the Project Manager's own, an administrator decides.
- **No project**: **your manager**, the **Manager** on your user record; when you have none, the **Finance Controllers**.

Either way, one person decides in their **Inbox**, never both. There is one step only: finance checks the money on the expenses afterwards, not on the request. **Requests to Approve** lists the requests waiting for you. Nobody decides their own request, and nobody sets **Approved** or **Rejected** by hand.

**Changed your mind while it waits?** Recall it from your Inbox. It goes back to **Draft**.

**A one-person organization.** Nobody approves a request they submitted, an administrator included, so a request needs a second user who can approve it: your manager, a Finance Controller or an administrator, or for a request on a project, the project's Project Manager or an administrator. Until that person has been added, a submitted request waits in **Submitted** and nobody can decide it: recall it from your Inbox, and submit it again once they have been added.

**Only a Draft request can be deleted.** Once it is sent it stays on record, with its approval and its expense reports. To call off an approved trip, choose **Cancel Trip**; a rejected request goes back to **Draft** first.

## After approval

The approval was given for the plan, so an approved request's **Purpose**, **Destination**, dates, **Estimated Amount** and **Project** no longer change. Its legs are held the same way from the moment you submit: their kind, places, dates and details no longer change, and no leg can be added or removed. To change the plan, choose **Cancel Trip** and file a new request. When the trip is over, choose **Close**.

The **Booking Reference** of each leg stays open, except while the request waits for approval: then every leg is held, its booking reference included, and a change is refused until the approval is decided. Once a leg is booked, you, an assistant working as you through the API, your approver or whoever books writes the confirmation there, also after approval.

## Reporting the trip

Send the trip's expenses with **Submit for a Trip** and pick this request ([Expenses](/docs/expenses#expenses-of-a-trip)). The request then reads its spending against the estimate:

| Field | What it shows |
| --- | --- |
| **Reported Amount** | The totals of its expense reports that are submitted, approved or paid. A draft or rejected report counts once it is sent again |
| **Difference from Estimate** | Reported minus estimated: above zero is over the estimate |
| **Over the Estimate** | Ticked when the reported amount is over the estimate |

Its expense reports are listed on its page, and each one shows its approver the same figures. Over the estimate is a sign for the approver, not a block.

When finance marks a report paid and no other report of the request is still draft, submitted or approved, the request is **Closed** by itself. A cancelled or closed request takes no new report.

## Who sees what

You see your own requests and their legs, and the ones you are asked to approve. Finance sees every request under **All Travel Requests**, and so do sales managers and leadership; none of them can change a request that is not theirs. Travel requests do not appear in the Sales, Service or Marketing apps, or on the account page.

With Project Delivery on, project staff also find **My Travel Requests** in the **Project Delivery** app, beside **My Expenses**, and a project's page lists its travel requests under **Related** (each reader sees the ones above). A travel request adds nothing to the project's cost: the cost arrives with the expenses. With Project Delivery off, the **Project** field is gone and every request goes to the traveler's manager.
