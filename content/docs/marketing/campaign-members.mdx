# Campaign Members

A **campaign member** is the link between a [campaign](/docs/marketing/campaigns) and a person (a [lead](/docs/sales/leads) or a [contact](/docs/sales/contacts)). It records that the campaign was sent to that person, and what came back: whether they responded, whether their lead went on to convert, or whether they asked to stop hearing from you.

## How members get enrolled

Three ways:

1. **Bulk enrollment, run from the campaign** — open a campaign and click **Enroll Members** in the record header; the action is offered while the campaign is *Planning* or *In Progress*. It opens the *Enrollment Criteria* screen of the [Campaign Enrollment flow](/docs/marketing/campaigns#campaign-enrollment-flow), where you pick the audience yourself — leads by status, or contacts by department — and it then creates a member per matching person with status *Sent*, skipping the already-enrolled and the opted-out. Nothing runs it for you: the criteria are answered on that screen, and a scheduled run would have nobody to answer them. A run that added everyone it could ends with *Eligible members enrolled in campaign.* A run in which anyone was refused ends with a refusal instead, giving both counts: how many people were enrolled and how many were refused. A refused person is not on the campaign.
2. **From a lead list or a contact list** — **Add to Campaign** ships on both. On **All Leads** or **All Contacts**, tick the rows you want and run it from the selection toolbar; on any list laid out in rows, you can also run it on a single row from that row's ⋮ menu. Either way you choose the campaign from a record picker. It is not a button in the list header, where nothing is selected yet. From the toolbar, a confirmation step lists the people you ticked, each one is enrolled on its own, and the result reads *Succeeded* with a count; from a row's menu, a confirmation toast follows. It is a list action on both objects, so opening a lead or a contact does not offer it. People already on that campaign are skipped, and the run still reports success for them, so re-running it never double-counts a touch — the skip-check is per relationship, so a person enrolled once as a lead and once as a contact is deliberately two memberships rather than a duplicate. There is still no *Add Members* picker on the campaign that reaches lead and contact list views.
3. **One at a time on the campaign** — open a campaign, find the **Campaign Members** panel on the *Related* tab, and use its **New** button to fill in a single membership by hand.

**Who can enroll.** Enrolling someone creates a campaign member, and all three ways check that one right, as the person doing it: their profile's permission to create campaign members. Marketing User and the administrators hold it. Sales Rep and Sales Manager do not: their profiles read campaign members and leave enrollment to marketing, so they are not offered **Enroll Members** or **Add to Campaign**, and a member they try to create anyway — through either of those, the panel's **New** or the API — is refused.

**Which campaigns, and which people.** A campaign takes members only while it is *Planning* or *In Progress*, and nobody who has opted out of email is enrolled. **Enroll Members** is offered only on such a campaign and leaves opted-out people out of its segment. **Add to Campaign** holds the same two rules, whichever way you start it. On a row's ⋮ menu its campaign picker lists only *Planning* and *In Progress* campaigns; the selection toolbar's picker lists every campaign. If a closed campaign or an opted-out person reaches it either way, nobody is added, the campaign's numbers do not move, and a message names the closed campaign or the person who opted out. So a *Completed* campaign keeps the sent, lead and response figures it closed with. The same two rules hold when a member is added any other way: **New** on a campaign's **Campaign Members** list, an import, or the API is refused with the same message, and nothing is saved. That message is shown in English in every language for now.

A person can be in many campaigns over time, but only once per campaign (one membership per person per campaign).

## The 4 member statuses

The member status is the response lifecycle, and every value on it has something in the app that writes it:

| Status | What it means | Written by |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ⚪ **Sent** | The campaign reached them *(the default for a new member, however it was created)* | Enrollment |
| 🟢 **Responded** | They answered — replied, registered, downloaded | **Mark Responded** on the member |
| 🟣 **Converted** | Their lead went on to become a customer | Automatic, when that lead is converted |
| 🔴 **Unsubscribed** | They asked to stop hearing from you | A user who may edit campaign members, on the member record |

These four are not a ladder that only climbs. Most members stay at *Sent*; one who answers moves to *Responded*, and a member still at *Sent* or *Responded* whose lead is later converted is promoted to *Converted* for you. Moving a member back — a mis-click corrected, or a reset to *Sent* for a re-send — clears the response stamp with it. Unsubscribing a member who had responded does not: the response stays on the member and still counts as one of the campaign's responses. An *Unsubscribed* member is never promoted by that conversion sweep: an opt-out is not overwritten by a marketing outcome.

## Why both lead and contact?

A campaign member can be linked to either a **lead** (someone not yet qualified) or a **contact** (someone at an existing account) — but not both. This is enforced by a built-in rule: exactly one of `crm_lead` or `crm_contact` must be set.

This dual support means:

- Marketing can target **prospects** (leads) for top-of-funnel campaigns.
- Marketing can target **existing customers** (contacts) for upsell, cross-sell or renewal campaigns.

If a lead is later converted to a contact, the existing campaign memberships stay linked to the lead, and new campaigns can re-target the same person as a contact.

## What the member record stores

| Field | Purpose |
| --- | --- |
| Member Number | The membership's own reference (CM-00001), generated on save |
| Campaign | The campaign — required |
| Lead | The lead, if the person was a prospect when they were enrolled |
| Contact | The contact, if the person is an existing contact |
| Added Date | When the person was enrolled — stamped automatically the moment the membership is saved, whichever of the three ways above created it, and read-only afterwards |
| Status | Where the person got to (the four above) |
| Response Date | When they responded, stamped the first time they reach a responded status |
| Has Responded | Whether the person ever responded, kept for you — *Converted* counts as responded, a reset to *Sent* clears it, and a member who responded keeps it after unsubscribing |

## What is tracked, and what is not

> **This app does not track opens, clicks or bounces.** The platform's email service sends mail and records what became of the message — *Queued → Sent*, or *Failed* — but it ships no tracking pixel, no click webhook and no bounce feed. A member status that depended on one would sit at its default forever, so none exists. What the app can observe is a person answering and a lead converting, and that is what the four statuses say.

Each status has a real writer:

- **Sent** — written when the membership is created, by every enrollment path above.
- **Responded** — the **Mark Responded** button on the member record. Recording a response edits the member, so the button is offered only to users who may edit campaign members (Marketing User and the administrators), and only while the member is still at *Sent*: an already-responded, converted or unsubscribed member has nothing to record, and an unsubscribed one must not be pulled back into the funnel by a mis-click. It stamps *Response Date* and *Has Responded* along with the status.
- **Converted** — automatic. When a member's lead is converted, every campaign that lead is enrolled in promotes its member row and recomputes its numbers. Conversion is the one campaign outcome the app observes by itself.
- **Unsubscribed** — set on the member record by a user who may edit campaign members (Marketing User and the administrators), usually when the person asks to stop hearing from you. Nothing in the app sets it on its own: an opt-out request reaches the member only when one of those users records it there. Setting it also ticks **Email Opt Out** on that person's lead or contact, which is what keeps them out of future campaigns: the enrollment flow skips opted-out people. It runs one way only — opting a person out does not rewrite the memberships they already have, because those record what happened at the time.

## How the metrics roll up

The campaign detail page rolls up its members:

```
Sent          = every member of the campaign
Responses     = members who ever responded (Has Responded), unsubscribed ones included
Response rate = Responses ÷ Sent, as a percentage
```

Conversions reach the campaign as its converted-lead count, and — once the opportunity carries the campaign — as the pipeline and ROI figures on the same page. Unsubscribes are not a campaign counter: you read them on the member rows, and on the person's *Email Opt Out*.

The numbers are recomputed on every membership change — someone enrolled, someone marked responded, someone removed — so a campaign that is still running reports real figures instead of waiting for a nightly job. Mark a member *Responded* and the campaign's response rate moves with it.

## Where members appear

On the campaign detail page, under the **Related** tab, in a panel titled **Campaign Members**. The panel lists every member of that campaign with five columns — **Lead**, **Contact**, **Added Date**, **Status** and **Response Date** — and carries the **New** button described above. The columns run in the order the row happens in: who was enrolled, when they were enrolled, and how they answered.

Two things readers arrive expecting, and where they really are:

- There is no *Members* tab. Campaign membership is one panel among the campaign's other related records on the shared *Related* tab.
- There are no saved member list views — not *All Members*, *Responded*, *Unsubscribed* or *Converted to Opportunity*. Campaign Member ships no list views at all, and a related-list panel would not read them if it did: the panel's columns are curated on the campaign link itself, never from a view. Nothing ships that narrows the panel to one status, and no report ships over Campaign Member either, so "everyone who responded" is a number on the campaign rather than a list you can open: see [How the metrics roll up](#how-the-metrics-roll-up), where the response rate is computed live from the members' responses.

## Tips for marketers

- ✅ **Watch the unsubscribes** — each one also opts that person out of email, so they drop out of every future enrollment automatically. A rising count is a signal to change the content, not the list.
- ✅ **Judge a campaign on *Responded* and *Converted*** — they are the two outcomes this app can actually observe.
- ✅ **Tag campaign source on opportunities** — without it the conversion won't roll up.

## Tips for sales reps

- ✅ When you open a lead, check **Campaign Memberships** in the sidebar — it tells you which campaigns they were sent and how they answered.
- ✅ Use the campaign as a conversation starter — *"I saw you joined our recent webinar on..."*

## Tips for admins

- There is no open/click tracking to switch on, and no connector that adds it. If a campaign's response numbers look low, check that marketing is recording replies with **Mark Responded** — that button, and a lead converting, are the only things that move a member off *Sent*. Sales reps and managers cannot press it: their profiles read campaign members without editing them.
- The rule that exactly one of *Lead* or *Contact* must be set lives in the campaign_member object's validation rules.
