# Files & Comments

Two things belong on a record and nowhere else: **the documents that prove what
was agreed**, and **the conversation about it**. HotCRM keeps both on the record,
so the contract is where the account is and the "why did we discount this?"
thread is where the deal is — not in someone's mailbox.

## 📎 Attachments

Open any account, contact, opportunity, quote, contract or case and you'll find
an **Attachments** panel below the record details.

| Object | Typical attachments |
| --- | --- |
| **Account** | Signed MSA, org chart, security questionnaire |
| **Contact** | Signed NDA, business card, meeting notes |
| **Opportunity** | Proposal, redlines, signed order form |
| **Quote** | Generated quote PDF, countersigned copy |
| **Contract** | The executed contract itself |
| **Case** | Screenshots, log bundles, repro steps |

### Uploading

1. Open the record.
2. Click **Upload** in the Attachments panel (or drop a file onto it).
3. The file appears in the list with its name, size and type.

Each row has a **download** and a **delete** action.

### Who can see and do what

Attachments have no permissions of their own — **they inherit the record's**.
That single rule answers every question:

- **Uploading** requires *edit* access to the record. If you cannot edit the
  opportunity, you cannot attach to it.
- **Seeing and downloading** requires *read* access to the record. A colleague
  who can open the deal sees its files; someone who cannot, does not — the
  file list comes back empty for them rather than teasing a locked row.
- **Deleting** is limited to the person who uploaded the file and anyone who
  can edit the record.
- **Anonymous visitors get nothing.** Someone filling in a public web-to-lead
  or web-to-case form is never handed a file, even with a direct link.

Because access follows the record, sharing a record — by territory, by account
team, or by changing the owner — carries its attachments along automatically.
There is no second permission to remember.

### Leads deliberately don't take attachments

Unqualified leads arrive from forms and lists in volume, and an attachment
surface on them mostly collects junk. Once a lead converts, its account,
contact and opportunity all accept files. If your team has a real reason to
attach to leads before qualification, ask your admin — it is one flag.

> A file **field** on a record (like a product datasheet) is a different thing
> from an attachment: the field holds exactly one file and is part of the
> record's data, while the Attachments panel holds as many as you like
> alongside it.

## 💬 Comments

Every record carries a **Discussion** feed: a comment box plus the record's
activity, newest at the bottom.

- Write in the box and press <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>Enter</kbd> (Mac:
  <kbd>⌘</kbd>+<kbd>Enter</kbd>) to post.
- Basic formatting is available — **bold**, *italic*, lists and code.
- Type <kbd>@</kbd> to mention a colleague; they get a notification.
- React to a comment with an emoji, or reply to start a thread.

System entries (a stage change, an owner change) appear in the same feed, so
the conversation and the record's history read as one story.

### What to put in a comment

- ✅ *"Procurement wants net-60; approved by finance, see attached MSA."*
- ✅ *"Champion left the company — new contact is Dana, re-qualifying."*
- ❌ Anything you would not want the whole revenue team to read. Comments are
  visible to your colleagues; treat them as internal notes on shared work,
  not as private messages.

## Tips

- ✅ Attach the document **at the moment it is signed** — the deal record is the
  only place everyone will look for it in six months.
- ✅ Put the *decision* in a comment, not just the outcome in a field. `Stage =
  Negotiation` says what; the comment says why.
- ✅ @-mention the person you need something from. A comment nobody is tagged in
  is a diary entry.
- ✅ Name files so they read in a list — `apex-msa-2026-signed.pdf` beats
  `scan_0043.pdf`.
