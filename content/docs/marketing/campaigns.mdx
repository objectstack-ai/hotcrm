# Campaigns

A **campaign** is any marketing initiative — an email blast, a webinar, a trade show booth, a content download, a paid social ad campaign. Each campaign has a budget, a target audience, a duration, and a measurable outcome (revenue, leads, opportunities).

## The 4 campaign statuses

| Status | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| ⚪ **Planning** | Being designed — audience, budget, content *(default)* |
| 🟠 **In Progress** | Live — members are enrolled, and the responses that come back are recorded on them |
| 🟢 **Completed** | Finished — the run is over; the metrics stay live |
| 🔴 **Aborted** | Cancelled before completion |

The system auto-moves campaigns from *In Progress* to *Completed* the day after their end date — so you don't have to remember.

## The 8 campaign types

| Type | Use for |
| --- | --- |
| **Email** *(default)* | Newsletters, drip programmes, broadcast emails |
| **Webinar** | Virtual events with registration and attendance tracking |
| **Trade Show** | In-person booth presence at industry events |
| **Conference** | Speaking slots, sponsorships |
| **Direct Mail** | Physical letters, gifts, packages |
| **Social Media** | Paid or organic social campaigns |
| **Content Marketing** | Blog posts, ebooks, whitepapers, gated content |
| **Partner Marketing** | Joint campaigns with channel partners |

And 5 primary channels: **Digital · Social · Email · Events · Partner**. Pick the channel that best describes how the campaign reaches the audience — it's used by the channel-mix report.

## What a campaign record stores

The campaign has no hand-built detail page: the record screen is derived from the object's own field groups, six sections in this order.

| Section | What is in it |
| --- | --- |
| **Campaign Information** | Campaign code, name, description, type, channel, status |
| **Schedule** | Start date, end date |
| **Budget & ROI** | Budgeted cost, actual cost, expected revenue, actual revenue *(rolled up)*, ROI *(calculated)* |
| **Performance** | Target size, the rollup counters, and the response rate *(calculated)* — see [Measuring a campaign](#measuring-a-campaign) |
| **Ownership** | Campaign owner |
| **Campaign Assets** | Landing page, and whether the campaign is active *(collapsed until you open it)* |

There is no *Engagement* section, and no open or click column anywhere on the campaign: what it rolls up is enrollments, responses, conversions, and the revenue attributed back to it.

The **Campaign Code** is auto-generated (e.g., CAMP-000123) so every campaign has a unique short reference for email subject lines and tracking URLs.

## Built-in rules

- **End date must be on or after the start date** — basic sanity check.
- **Over-budget warning** — if actual cost exceeds budgeted cost, a warning shows on the record. Doesn't block saves, but flags the campaign for finance review.

## What happens automatically

- **Auto-complete** — at 2 AM daily, any *In Progress* campaign past its end date is marked *Completed*. The sweep flips the status and only the status — the numbers were already current before it ran.
- **Live metrics** — the metric block (sent, responded, converted, ROI) is recomputed as members are enrolled, as members respond, as their leads convert, and as opportunities are attributed to the campaign. A campaign you are running right now reports real numbers; you do not wait for it to finish to read them.
- **ROI calculation** — the campaign's actual revenue is the rolled-up sum of related closed-won opportunity amounts. ROI = (actual revenue − actual cost) ÷ actual cost.

Member enrollment is deliberately not on that list: nothing enrolls members when a campaign goes *In Progress*, and no schedule enrolls them later. It is a button someone presses — see [Campaign enrollment flow](#campaign-enrollment-flow) below.

## Campaign enrollment flow

Bulk enrollment is a step you run, not one that happens to you. Open the campaign and click **Enroll Members** in the record header — the action is offered while the campaign is *Planning* or *In Progress* — and the flow opens its *Enrollment Criteria* screen on that campaign.

You choose the audience on that screen:

1. **Enroll** — *Leads* or *Contacts*. One run enrolls one side, not both.
2. The segment on that side — leads by **status**, contacts by **department**. Both fields carry a default, so you change the one that matches the side you picked.

The flow then takes everyone eligible in that segment — they have an email address, they are not opted out of email, and a lead must not already be converted — and creates a [campaign member](/docs/marketing/campaign-members) per person with status *Sent*. Anyone already on the campaign is skipped, so running it again tops the campaign up instead of counting a touch twice.

There is no schedule behind this, and the criteria are the reason. They are answered on the screen, and a scheduled run would arrive with nobody to answer them — no campaign, no segment — so enrolling is deliberately something a person does.

For real-time campaigns (e.g., a webinar with a registration form), you can also add members **manually** from the campaign detail page or via the **Add to Campaign** action on any lead/contact.

## Measuring a campaign

The numbers live on the campaign record itself — the counters and the response rate in *Performance*, the money and ROI in *Budget & ROI*. Every one of them is recomputed as members are enrolled or answer, as their leads convert, and as opportunities are attributed to the campaign:

- **Number Sent** — every member enrolled in the campaign, whatever their status.
- **Number of Responses** — members at *Responded* **or** *Converted*: a member whose lead converted answered too, and the counter says so.
- **Response Rate %** — responses ÷ number sent, as a percentage. It is the only rate the campaign carries.
- **Number of Leads** — the distinct leads among those members.
- **Converted Leads** — how many of those leads have since been converted.
- **Opportunities Created** and **Won Opportunities** — opportunities naming this campaign as their source, and how many of them closed won.
- **Actual Revenue** — the sum of those won opportunities' amounts. Open pipeline is not summed anywhere on the campaign; closed-won is the only money it rolls up.
- **ROI %** — (actual revenue − actual cost) ÷ actual cost, as a percentage.

> **A campaign has no open rate, click rate or conversion rate.** Its members are *Sent*, *Responded*, *Converted* or *Unsubscribed* — four values with no order between them, so "the members at or above *Opened*" is not a question this app can be asked; opens, clicks and bounces are not tracked at all, and [Campaign Members](/docs/marketing/campaign-members) explains why. Conversion reaches the campaign as the **Converted Leads** and **Opportunities Created** counts, never as a percentage.

## Attribution: from campaign to revenue

The link is the **Source Campaign** lookup on the opportunity. When a sales rep converts a lead that came from a campaign — or manually sets the source campaign on an opportunity — the system credits the revenue back to the campaign.

For deals influenced by multiple campaigns (multi-touch), the convention is to record the **last-touch** campaign (the one closest to conversion). For full multi-touch attribution, consider extending the model with a campaign-influence join object — see [Customization](/docs/customization/extending-objects).

## Standard list views

Campaigns saves **four** views (`src/views/campaign.view.ts`), and the tab row above the list changes how the same campaigns are *drawn*, not which campaigns are in them. One tab per view, each labelled with the view's own name:

| View | What it shows |
| --- | --- |
| **All Campaigns** | The grid: name (pinned left, and the link into the record), channel, status, start and end date, budgeted cost and expected revenue — the last two with column totals. Ordered by **Start Date**, earliest first. 25 rows a page. |
| **Campaign Schedule** | One gantt bar per campaign, from start date to end date, titled by name, so overlapping runs line up against one another. |
| **Launch Calendar** | The same campaigns on a month calendar, placed on their **Start Date** and coloured by channel. |
| **Marketing Timeline** | The same start-to-end spans on a month scale, grouped by channel and coloured by status. |

None of the four filters anything, so **every campaign you can see appears in all four** and switching tabs only redraws them. There is no saved *Active Campaigns*, *Upcoming Campaigns*, *Completed This Quarter*, *Over Budget*, *By Type* or *Best ROI*: to narrow the list to one status, sort and filter the grid columns yourself. **ROI** is not among those columns — it is on the campaign record, under *Budget & ROI*, and in the marketing dashboards.

## Sharing — campaigns are public by default

Campaigns are **public read-only** for the whole organisation. Anyone in sales can see what marketing is running, and use that context in customer conversations. Edit access is reserved for marketing users.

## Tips for marketers

- ✅ **Set the expected revenue** at launch — it's how finance forecasts the campaign's contribution.
- ✅ **Record actual cost as soon as you know it** — over-budget warnings can't help if the field is empty.
- ✅ **Update the campaign status to *Completed*** if you finish early; don't wait for the auto-complete.
- ✅ **Document what worked** in the description — future you and future colleagues will thank you.

## Tips for sales reps

- ✅ When a lead arrives, check whether they're in a campaign — it tells you which campaigns they were sent, and how they answered.
- ✅ Set **Source Campaign** on opportunities you closed — marketing can't measure ROI without this.

## Tips for admins

- The auto-complete cron (2 AM daily) is configurable. There is no enrollment cron to configure — enrollment is the **Enroll Members** screen flow, which someone runs from a campaign. See [Administration › Automation](/docs/administration/automation).
- To add a new campaign type or channel, edit the campaign object's picklists. See [Customization › Extending Objects](/docs/customization/extending-objects).
- For multi-touch attribution, see the customisation guide.
