# Contracts

A **contract** is the legal agreement between your company and a customer — what they buy, for how long, at what price, on what payment terms. In HotCRM, contracts are the **system of record for active customer relationships**: once activated, they unlock the *Customer* status on the account.

## The 5 statuses

| Status | Color | What it means |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ⚪ **Draft** | Gray | Being negotiated, not signed |
| 🔵 **In Approval** | Blue | Sent for internal sign-off |
| 🟢 **Activated** | Green | Signed and in force |
| 🟠 **Expired** | Orange | End date has passed |
| 🔴 **Terminated** | Red | Cancelled early |

Activation is the key transition — it triggers the account to be promoted to *Customer*.

## The contract form: order, framework or other

Every contract has a **Contract Form**, which decides where its value comes from. It sits beside the contract type and does not replace it: the type says *what* is agreed (a subscription, a service, an NDA), the form says what the contract is *for*.

| Form | How it is made | Its value |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Order Contract** | **Draft Contract** on an order — never by hand | The order's total, tax included. Read-only on the form |
| **Framework Contract** | By hand — the default for a new contract | Typed by hand. It may carry many orders |
| **Other** | By hand | No amount needed, such as an NDA. The value is 0 unless you type one |

You choose **Framework Contract** or **Other** while the contract is a draft; once it leaves Draft, the form is fixed. An order contract stays an order contract, and its **Source Order** names the order it was drafted from.

**An order contract follows its order while it is a draft.** Its **Order Amount** and its **Contract Value** are the order's total, whatever the order's status but Cancelled: change the order (or the quote it came from), and the draft contract's value changes with it. Nobody types the value of an order contract.

**Once the contract is activated, its value stays as signed.** If the order's amount changes afterwards, the contract shows **Differs from the Order Amount**, and its value does not move until someone runs **Change** with **Update from Order** (below).

Contracts that existed before contract forms took one by this rule, the first time each was saved after the upgrade: a contract drafted by **Draft Contract** and named by its order is an order contract; a contract with no value is **Other**; every other contract is a framework contract. An order contract whose value already differed from its order was left as it was, showing **Differs from the Order Amount**, for the business to check.

## Framework contract quota

A framework contract's value is the ceiling for the orders placed under it. Its **Order Totals** section shows how much of it the orders use:

- **Order Amount** — the total of the **Confirmed** and **Completed** orders linked to the contract, tax included. A Draft is not yet ordered and a Cancelled order never will be, so neither counts — here, in Remaining Quota, or in the contract's Unpaid and Uninvoiced ([Order to Cash](/docs/order-to-cash)). Confirming a Draft adds it.
- **Remaining Quota** — the contract value less the Order Amount. Below zero, the orders have gone over the contract value.

**Going over warns, and the save goes through.** Linking an order to a framework contract, or raising a linked order's total, so that the orders under it total more than the contract value saves the order and shows a warning. A Draft order is checked as if it were confirmed, so the warning comes while there is still time to change it, and confirming an order that takes the contract over warns again. An edit that does not add to the order's total — or lowers it — gives no warning, so an order already over is not flagged again on every save. An order line that takes its order over saves without the warning; the contract's Remaining Quota shows it.

The quota is the contract's current value. When **Change** gives an activated framework contract a new value, Remaining Quota counts from the new one. Order and Other contracts have no quota: an order contract's value *is* its order's amount, and an Other contract has none.

## Changing an activated contract

On an **Activated** contract, the **Contract Value**, the **End Date** and the **Payment Terms** are read-only on the form. They change only through **Change**, on the contract's header:

- Give a **Change Reason** and upload the **Signed Amendment** the customer signed. Change without both does not save.
- A framework (or other) contract may be given a **New Contract Value**. An order contract is offered no value at all: tick **Update from Order** to take the order's current amount.
- A **New End Date** and **New Payment Terms** are optional. A field left empty keeps its value. When the new end date takes the term out of step with **Contract Term (Months)**, the term is counted again from the dates.

The contract stays **Activated**, so renewal reminders and the expiration sweep go on as before. Change creates no change order, does not change the order, and does not re-plan the payments. The contract's **History** shows each value before and after, who changed it and when, and the **Last Change** section shows the latest reason and amendment.

## The 6 contract types

| Type | Use for |
| --- | --- |
| **Subscription** | Recurring access — SaaS, software subs |
| **Service** | Time-bounded services — consulting, training |
| **License** | Perpetual software licenses |
| **Partnership** | Reseller, referral, channel partner agreements |
| **NDA** | Non-disclosure (zero-value) |
| **MSA** | Master Service Agreement — umbrella for many SOWs |

## What a contract record stores

A contract record declares its fields in **eight groups**. The contract form shows a curated subset of them across its tabs, so read this as the full inventory of what a contract carries rather than a screen-by-screen map:

| Group | What's in it |
| --- | --- |
| **Contract Information** | Contract Name, Contract Form, Source Order, Contract Number *(auto-numbered `CTR-0001`)*, Contract Type, Contract Document, Description |
| **Parties** | Contract Owner, Account, Primary Contact, Related Opportunity — the contact and opportunity pickers are scoped to the account you pick first |
| **Terms & Dates** | Contract Term (Months), Start Date, End Date, Special Terms |
| **Contract Value** | Contract Value, Billing Frequency, Payment Terms, Billing Address |
| **Order Totals** | Order Amount *(order and framework contracts)*, Differs from the Order Amount *(order contracts)*, Remaining Quota *(framework contracts)* — what the orders add up to; not printed on the contract |
| **Status & Approval** | Status, Signed Date, Signed By, Signed Copy |
| **Renewal** | Auto Renewal, Renewal Notice (Days) |
| **Last Change** | Change Reason, Signed Amendment — written by **Change** |

Contract Name, Contract Form, Account, Primary Contact, Contract Term, Start Date, End Date, Contract Value and Status are all **required** — a contract cannot be saved without them. Contract Value starts at 0, so an Other contract needs no amount typed.

## The contract's name and number

A contract's **Contract Name** is what people call it — *Annual Service 2027*, *Phase 2 Implementation* — and it is the contract's title. The record header, the first column of the **All Contracts** list, the **Contracts** tab on an account, an opportunity or a contact, approvals and notices all show it, so two contracts of one customer read apart. The new-contract form asks for it first.

The **Contract Number** stays: the system numbers every contract (`CTR-0001`) and never reuses a number. It stands beside the name — in the list's second column and first in the record header's strip — and wherever you pick a contract (on a plan step, an invoice or a payment) each candidate shows its number under its name. Search finds a contract by either. The contract notices name it by both: *Contract drafted: CTR-0006 · Annual Service 2027*.

A drafted contract takes its source's name, which you can change like any other field: **Draft Contract** on an order uses the order's name, which an order created from an accepted quote carries over from the quote; the **Accepted Quote Drafts a Contract** flow, when switched on, uses the quote's name, or the opportunity's when the quote has none.

Contracts saved before contracts had names are named once, after the upgrade, by an administrator (see the release notes): each gets its source quote's name, or otherwise the account's name and its start date — *Acme Corporation 2026-11-24*.

Three field names people go looking for and do not find:

- **Activation Date.** There is none, and no other field records the moment of activation. Activation stamps **Signed Date** instead (see *On contract activation* below), so that is the date any "when did this go live" report has to read.
- **Renewal Terms.** Renewal is exactly the two fields above. Free-text renewal instructions belong in **Special Terms**, which travels with the contract.
- **Account Exec.** Ownership is the single **Contract Owner** lookup; a contract carries no second ownership field.

**Contract Document** is a **link**, not an upload — it holds a URL pointing at wherever the document lives in another system. The contract HotCRM prints goes on the record as an **attachment**, and the copy the customer signs and sends back goes in **Signed Copy**, an upload — see *Printing a contract and the signed copy* below. There is no separate *Order Form* field.

## Printing a contract and the signed copy

**Print Contract**, on the contract's record page and in the contract list's row menu, prints the contract as a document and files the PDF under the contract's **Attachments**, in your name. The document carries the parties (contract name, number and type, account, primary contact and contract owner), the **Terms & Dates**, **Contract Value** and **Renewal** groups, and a signature block for each side; each sheet repeats the header and carries its page number. Printing changes nothing else on the contract, so a contract can be printed as often as it needs to be. The button is offered to whoever may edit the contract and attach files to it — a **Sales Manager** or a **System Administrator**, not a **Sales Rep** (see *Who can edit which contract* below).

What the customer signs and sends back is uploaded to **Signed Copy**, in the **Status & Approval** group (on the Edit form, the **Signing & Documents** tab). The save that files it fills **Signed Date** with today's date when Signed Date is empty; a date already on the contract, or typed in the same save, is kept.

**Activated requires the signed copy.** The save that moves a contract to *Activated* is refused on **Signed Copy** — *"Signed Copy is required"* — until a file is there, whether it comes from the Edit form or the REST API (`400 VALIDATION_FAILED`). An *Activated* contract cannot have its signed copy removed either: that save is refused the same way. A contract that was already *Activated* without a signed copy when this rule arrived is not touched by it on the server: an import, an integration or the REST API saves its other edits, and it still expires or is terminated as before. The console's Edit form is stricter: it asks for **Signed Copy** before it saves any change to such a contract, so upload the copy first. Printing needs a print engine on the server (`OS_PRINT_CHROMIUM_PATH` or `OS_PRINT_BROWSER_ENDPOINT`, plus `OS_PRINT_CONSOLE_URL`); without one, Print Contract reports the error and files nothing.

## Billing frequency

How often the customer is invoiced — four values, and a new contract starts on **Monthly** unless you change it:

- **Monthly** *(default)*
- **Quarterly**
- **Annually**
- **One-time**

There is no *Custom* billing frequency. A cadence that is none of these four has to be written out in **Special Terms**; the picklist will not hold it.

## Payment terms

When the customer pays — five values, the same five a quote offers, so the two records speak one vocabulary:

- **Net 15** — within 15 days of invoice
- **Net 30** *(default)* — within 30 days
- **Net 60**
- **Net 90**
- **Due on Receipt** — immediately

There is no *Net 45* and no *Prepaid*. Payment upfront is an arrangement you record in **Special Terms**, not a term you can pick here.

## Renewals

Two fields drive renewals:

- **Auto Renewal** — off unless you turn it on. With it on, the daily renewal sweep opens the renewal deal for you; with it off, the sweep still reminds you but creates no deal.
- **Renewal Notice (Days)** — how many days before the end date the reminder fires. Defaults to 30, and each contract carries its own.

There is no *Renewal Terms* field. Instructions like "renew at 5% uplift unless the customer opts out" go in **Special Terms**, the free-text field on the contract itself.

When a contract enters its renewal window, the system files a follow-up task and emails the contract owner, on a deployment that runs scheduled work — see *Daily — renewal reminder* below for everything it creates.

## What the system does automatically

### On contract activation
Changing a contract's status **to** *Activated* runs two writes, and only these two:

1. **Signed Date** stamped on the contract — today's date, and only when the contract has none yet. Set Signed Date in the same edit that activates the contract and your date is kept. Filing the **Signed Copy** has usually filled it already (see *Printing a contract and the signed copy* above).
2. **Account type → Customer** — every account that is not already a *Customer* is promoted, so this catches a *Partner* or a *Former Customer* just as it catches a *Prospect*.

Nothing is sent and nobody is told. Activation raises no notification for the contract owner or the account owner, and no message goes to the primary contact — there is no outbound customer email here, and no setting behind one. There is no *Customer Since* field on the account either: an account keeps no date of its own for when it became a customer, so cohort reporting has to read the contract's signed date or start date.

Both writes run *after* the save, with failures logged rather than raised, so the contract stays activated even if promoting the account fails. They also hang off an **update** — a contract that arrives already *Activated* (a data import, seed data, an integration that writes the record in one shot) triggers neither, and its account stays a *Prospect*.

### Daily — expiration sweep
This sweep and the renewal reminder below are scheduled flows, so they run only on a deployment that has switched scheduled work on (see [Administration › Automation](/docs/administration/automation#scheduled-automation)). A default install has scheduled work off: there, no contract expires on its own, and an *Activated* contract past its end date stays *Activated* until someone changes its status.

Where it runs, each night at midnight the system finds *Activated* contracts whose end date has passed, and does two things to each — only these two:

1. **Status → Expired**.
2. **Notification to the contract owner** — inbox and email, linking to the contract.

Nobody else is told. There is no second notification: the account owner, the primary contact and the rest of the account team hear nothing when a contract lapses, so if anyone beyond the contract owner needs to know, an admin has to add that. The sweep also reads *Activated* contracts only — one still sitting in *Draft* or *In Approval* past its end date is left alone — and it takes at most 500 contracts per run.

### Daily — renewal reminder
Where scheduled work runs (see above), at 8 AM the system finds *Activated* contracts whose end date falls inside that contract's own *Renewal Notice (Days)* window, and for each one, in this order:

1. **Files a renewal task** for the contract owner — *"Renewal due: contract CTR-0042"*, a high-priority follow-up due on the contract's end date, filed against the account.
2. **Notifies the contract owner** — inbox and email. The notification links to the contract record, and that is all it does: there is no one-click action on it, and opening it creates nothing.
3. **Opens a renewal opportunity** — but only when *Auto Renewal* is on. An *Existing Customer - Renewal* deal on the same account, at *Proposal* stage, amount copied from the contract value, close date set to the contract's end date, owner copied from the contract, next step *"Confirm renewal terms with customer"*. It is skipped when that account already has an open renewal deal.

So the renewal deal is never something you click for. With *Auto Renewal* on it is already in the pipeline by the time you read the reminder; with it off, nobody opens one for you and the task is the whole of it.

The task is also what keeps the sweep from repeating itself: the same contract matches every morning of its notice window, and an open *Renewal due* task for it means the day is already handled — no second task, no second notification, no second deal. Complete that task and the next morning's sweep files a fresh one, for as long as the contract is still inside its window.

On a deployment without scheduled work none of this happens: no renewal task, no notification and no renewal deal. What still shows a renewal coming is the contract itself, through the **Renewal Calendar** tab and the **End Date** order of All Contracts (see *Standard list views* below).

Two bounds worth knowing: the sweep looks 120 days ahead at most, so **Renewal Notice (Days)** accepts at most 120 and a save asking for more is refused (a contract that already held a longer window keeps it, and is reminded at 120 days out); and it takes at most 500 contracts per run.

### Manual termination
Terminating a contract is a status change to *Terminated*, and two things about that change are worth knowing.

**Being the owner is not what qualifies you to make it.** Edit on Contract is an object-level right that ownership does not extend: **Sales Manager** and **System Administrator** hold it, **Sales Rep** does not. That gap lands on the most common case, because the contract an accepted quote drafts carries the quote's owner across with it — so the rep whose deal it was ends up owning a contract they can read and cannot touch. Terminating it is someone else's action to take — a Sales Manager's, on any edition, since a manager can edit every account and therefore every contract; see *Who can edit which contract* under Sharing below.

**And it is final.** An edit that moves a terminated contract to any other status is refused, and the contract stays *Terminated* — see *The status progression is a gate* below; a replacement is a new contract. The rest of the contract stays editable: a change to its notes, its terms or its primary contact saves as long as the status is left as it is.

**Status** is one of the fields Contract keeps history on, so the audit log does record who moved it and when. The others are the owner, the start and end dates, the value, the payment terms and the change reason.

## Built-in rules

Six rules reject a save outright:

- **End date must be after start date** — a validation rule on the object.
- **The term must match the dates** — the contract hook counts the months between **Start Date** and **End Date** and rejects anything more than **one month** away from **Contract Term (Months)**: *"Contract term (12 months) does not match date range (6 months from … to …)."* Term and dates move together, or the save bounces.
- **An activated contract changes only through Change** — once the contract is *Activated*, an edit to **Contract Value**, **End Date** or **Payment Terms** is refused, naming the field (*"An activated contract's End Date changes only through Change, with a reason and the signed amendment."*). See *Changing an activated contract* above.
- **An order contract's value is its order's** — typing **Contract Value** on an order contract is refused, whatever its status.
- **Activated requires a signed copy** — a save that moves a contract to *Activated*, or creates one already *Activated*, without a **Signed Copy** is refused on that field; see *Printing a contract and the signed copy* above.
- **The status progression is a gate.** The route is *Draft* → *In Approval* → *Activated* → *Expired* or *Terminated*, with *In Approval* able to go back to *Draft*, *Terminated* reachable from all three live statuses, and *Expired* and *Terminated* both final: a renewal is a new contract, never a revived one. A status change off that route — *Draft* straight to *Activated*, *Activated* back to *Draft*, or reviving an *Expired* or *Terminated* contract — is refused on **Status** with *"This status change is not allowed. A contract moves Draft → In Approval → Activated → Expired; In Approval can go back to Draft, and Draft, In Approval or Activated can be Terminated. Expired and Terminated are final: a renewal is a new contract."*, and the contract keeps its status, whether the change comes from the Edit form or the REST API (`400 VALIDATION_FAILED`). Only a change of the status is judged: any other edit saves on any contract, an expired or terminated one included. A contract *created* directly as *Activated* or *Expired* is not checked by this rule, which is the same import path that skips the activation writes described above; one created *Activated* still needs its **Signed Copy**.

One more thing is often read as a rule and is not:

- **Contract Value cannot be negative** — that is the field's own minimum of 0 rather than a validation rule, so NDAs at $0 are fine. **Contract Term (Months)** is bounded the same way, at a minimum of 1.

And one rule people expect that is only half there: **nothing stops an activated contract from being deleted directly.** Contract has no delete hook and no status check of its own — who may delete a contract is decided by their profile alone, and the profile never looks at the status. A **System Administrator** can delete any contract, activated ones included; **Sales Manager** and **Sales Rep** can delete none, not even a draft. What *is* refused is deleting the **account** out from under its contracts. Contracts belong to their account (master-detail, so an account delete would cascade them), and the account guard refuses to delete a customer account while an *Activated* contract still references it — *"Cannot delete customer account: 1 activated contract still references it. Terminate or reassign it first."* An account carrying only drafts or ended contracts is refused too, by the platform rather than the guard: the delete cascades the account's contacts first and stops on the contract's required **Primary Contact** (*"This Contact is still referenced by 1 Contract record(s) through Primary Contact, which is required and cannot be cleared"*). In practice an account with contracts cannot be deleted until its contracts are gone. If signed agreements must be undeletable outright in your org, an admin has to author that — the app does not do it for you.

## Sharing

Contracts are **Controlled by Parent** under the account: you see the contracts of every account you can see, by whichever route the account reached you — you own it, a territory or team sharing rule granted it, or someone shared it with you by hand. No sharing rule is authored on Contract itself, and none is needed; the account's rules carry its contracts (see [Sharing & Security](/docs/administration/sharing-and-security), *Controlled by Parent, in practice*). A rep who reached the account through a territory rule therefore sees its *Contracts* related list complete, including the renewals — the gap that #549 reported.

Reading is one door; editing is two:

- **Object level first.** Edit on Contract is a right on your profile that record visibility never extends: **Sales Manager** and **System Administrator** hold it, **Sales Rep** does not. A rep reads every contract on the accounts they can see — the one drafted from their own accepted quote included — and cannot change any of them.
- **Then the account.** Changing, adding or removing a contract requires **edit** access to its account, resolved the same way as reads. Ownership of the contract row itself is irrelevant.

### Who can edit which contract

| Profile | Can edit… | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **System Administrator** | any contract | *Modify All* on Contract — the super-user bypass |
| **Sales Manager** | any contract | holds *Modify All* on **Account**, so every account — and every contract under it — is editable; deleting a contract stays refused (no delete right on Contract) |
| **Sales Rep** | none | no object-level edit right at all — the first door is shut |

That middle row is the common case, and it holds on every edition: the contract drafted from an accepted quote's order belongs to the **rep**, and the manager changes it because they can edit its account, not because of any reporting relationship. (Earlier releases declared a hierarchy write depth here that only the Enterprise edition could resolve, so on the open edition a manager reached only their own contracts; that boundary is gone with the parent-derived model.)

## Standard list views

Contracts saves **one** list — **All Contracts**, every contract you may see, with no filter on it — and the tab row along the top of that list changes how the same records are *drawn*, not which records are in them. Four tabs, and nothing else:

| Tab | How it draws the same contracts |
| --- | --- |
| **All Contracts** *(the landing tab)* | The grid: contract name (pinned left, and the link into the record), contract number, account, related opportunity, status, contract value with a column total, term in months, start and end date, auto-renewal and owner. Ordered by **End Date**, soonest first, so whatever expires next is on top. 25 rows a page. |
| **Renewal Calendar** | The same contracts on a month calendar, each placed on its **End Date**, titled by contract name and coloured by status. |
| **Contract Terms** | One gantt bar per contract, from start date to end date, so overlapping terms line up against one another. |
| **Contract Timeline** | The same start-to-end spans on a quarter scale, grouped by account and coloured by status. |

None of the four filters anything, so **every contract you can see appears in all four** and switching tabs only redraws them. There is no saved *My Active Contracts*, *Expiring in 60 Days*, *Up for Renewal*, *Expired This Month* or *Pending Activation* — to narrow the list to your own contracts or to one status, sort and filter the grid columns yourself. What watches renewal dates for you, where scheduled work runs, is the daily renewal reminder described above, not a list view.

## Tips for contract owners

- ✅ **Upload the signed copy before you activate** — Activation waits for it, and the upload fills Signed Date for you.
- ✅ **Set Renewal Notice (Days) = 90** for enterprise deals — gives you a full quarter to negotiate, and the sweep looks far enough ahead to honour it.
- ✅ **Record what you agreed in Special Terms** — "price-locked for 3 years; 5% uplift on renewal". There is no *Renewal Terms* field, and Special Terms is the note that will still be on the contract when the reminder lands.

## Tips for sales reps

- ✅ **You never fill a contract in — the system drafts it.** Contract is read-only for a Sales Rep: you cannot create one and you cannot edit one. What you do own is the quote and its order: **Draft Contract** on the order drafts the contract — a *Draft* contract carrying the order's name, account, primary contact, related opportunity, owner, payment terms and total, on a 12-month term starting today, typed *Subscription* (see [Orders](/docs/sales/orders#drafting-the-contract-from-an-order)). Where an administrator has switched the **Accepted Quote Drafts a Contract** flow on, marking the quote **Accepted** drafts it instead, as every acceptance did before this release.
- ✅ **The opportunity link is copied off the quote, not pre-filled on a form you are typing into** — so there is nothing on it for you to keep or lose. Nor does the manual route pre-fill it: when a manager or an admin creates a contract by hand, the opportunity is picked by hand, and the only help the picker gives is that it lists the deals of the account already chosen on the contract. Nothing reads that link for reporting today — HotCRM ships no contract report or dashboard at all.
- ✅ **You read the contracts of your accounts and ask for the rest.** Contracts follow the account: every account you can see — your own, and the ones a territory or team rule shares with you — shows you its contracts, renewals included. Changing a term, a date, the value or the status is somebody else's job, and so is an amendment — that is a new contract, which only they can create. Ask your **Sales Manager**: a manager can change the contract standing in your name without it being handed over first, on any edition — see *Who can edit which contract* above.

## Tips for admins

- Contract activation is an **object hook** (`src/sales/objects/contract.hook.ts`), not a flow — that is where the signed-date stamp and the account promotion live, and it is not in the flow list. The expiration sweep and the renewal reminder *are* scheduled flows, so they run only once the deployment switches scheduled work on. See [Administration › Automation](/docs/administration/automation#scheduled-automation).
- Contract types and billing frequency picklists are extensible.
