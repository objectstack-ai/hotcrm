# Leads

A **lead** is an inbound enquiry — someone who has shown interest but hasn't yet been confirmed as a real sales opportunity. Leads are kept separate from your real accounts and contacts until you've decided they're worth pursuing.

## When you'd use a lead (vs. a contact)

| Scenario | Use a… |
| --- | --- |
| Someone filled in a web form | Lead |
| A name on a trade-show list | Lead |
| You bought a marketing list | Lead |
| Existing customer's new buyer joined | Contact (under the existing account) |
| Confirmed decision-maker at a real account | Contact |

The point is to keep unqualified noise out of your real customer database.

## The 5 lead statuses

Every lead moves through these states. Reps can only advance one step at a time — the system blocks invalid jumps.

| Status | What it means | What you typically do next |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **New** | Just arrived, nobody has reached out | Make first contact within SLA |
| **Contacted** | A rep has reached out by phone/email | Apply BANT qualification |
| **Qualified** | Passes BANT — worth pursuing | **Convert** to an account, contact and opportunity |
| **Unqualified** | Not a fit (wrong industry, no budget, etc.) | Optionally reopen later if things change |
| **Converted** | Has become a real account & contact | (Lead is now locked) |

You can also **reopen** an unqualified lead back to *New* if circumstances change (budget freed up, new project announced).

## What each lead record stores

A lead record declares its fields in **10 groups**. The lead's own record page curates its **Details** tab from a subset of them, so read this as the full inventory of what a lead carries rather than a screen-by-screen map:

| Group | What's in it |
| --- | --- |
| **Identity** | Salutation, first/last name, full name, display title |
| **Company Information** | Company, job title, industry — plus a hidden normalised copy of the company name, which is what conversion and duplicate checks match on |
| **Contact Information** | Email, phone, mobile, website |
| **Qualification** | Status, Lead Score (a 1-5 star rating), lead source, next follow-up date, last contacted, disqualification reason |
| **Assignment** | Owner |
| **Address** | A single structured address field |
| **Additional Info** | Annual revenue, number of employees, description, notes |
| **Communication Preferences** | Do-not-call, email opt-out |
| **Conversion** | Converted flag, converted account, contact and opportunity, converted date — all auto-filled when the lead is converted |
| **Duplicate Management** | Duplicate Of (which object the surviving record lives on), Duplicate Of Lead, Duplicate Of Contact, Duplicate Status — the fields behind *Duplicate leads* below |

The available **industries** include Technology, Software/SaaS, Finance, Healthcare, Retail, Manufacturing, Education, Real Estate, Media, Logistics, Hospitality, Energy, Government, Non-profit, and Other.

The available **lead sources** include Web, Referral, Event, Trade Show, Webinar, Partner, Advertisement, Cold Call, and Other.

## Lead scoring — what the score means

A lead carries exactly **one** quality number: **Lead Score**, a **1-5 star rating**. It is the same field the list views show in the *Score* column — there is no separate 0-100 number, and no budget or timeline field behind it.

### Where the stars come from

When a lead is created **without** a rating, the system works one out from the lead's own fields and stores it. Everything it looks at is on the record in front of you — there is no web-visit or engagement tracking behind the number:

| What it looks at | Stars added |
| --- | --- |
| A business email address — anything that isn't gmail, yahoo, hotmail, outlook, qq or 163 | +1 |
| Any phone number at all | +0.5 |
| A senior job title — CEO, CTO, CFO, CIO, COO, Founder, VP, Vice President, Director, Head of | +1.5 |
| Industry is Technology, Finance or Healthcare | +1 |
| 200 employees or more | +0.5 |
| Annual revenue of $10M or more | +0.5 |

The total is capped at 5, floored at 1 and rounded to a **whole star**: a lead is never scored 0, and half stars are never stored.

Two limits worth knowing:

- It runs **on creation only**. Filling in the job title or the headcount later does *not* re-score the lead — re-rate it yourself if the picture changed.
- It never overwrites a rating that came in with the lead. Import a lead with a rating, or set one on the create form, and your value is what is kept.

### What the stars trigger

New-lead routing reads the rating once, as the lead lands:

| Rating | Next Follow-up Date it stamps | Alert to the lead's owner |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **4 stars or better** | **Tomorrow** | *"Hot lead — assign within 24h"*, sent to both the **inbox and email**, flagged as a warning |
| **Everything else**, including a lead with no rating at all | **Three days out** | *"New lead to assign"*, **inbox only** |

Four stars is the whole of the hot threshold: a 5-star lead is routed exactly like a 4-star one, and nothing separate happens at five.

> The weights above live in the lead's automation code, not in a setup screen — see the admin tips at the end of this page.

## Duplicate leads

The same person enquiring twice is normal, not an error. A returning prospect who fills in the web form again — or a trade-show list that overlaps one you already imported — creates a **second lead**, and HotCRM records the relationship instead of refusing the record.

When a lead arrives with an email address already in the system, it is saved with:

- **Duplicate Status = Suspected** — a machine's guess, nothing has been decided.
- **Duplicate Of** — the record it appears to repeat: an existing **Contact** if the person already became one, otherwise the **oldest open Lead** with that address. Every re-submission points at the same original, so a cluster is one list, not a chain.

Work them from the **Suspected Duplicates** view in the Leads list's view switcher. Open the pair, compare them, and then either:

- **It's a new enquiry from a known person** — clear Duplicate Status and work the lead normally. A returning prospect with a fresh budget is a real opportunity.
- **It's the same enquiry twice** — set the lead to *Unqualified* with the reason **Duplicate**. The form then requires you to name the surviving record and set Duplicate Status to **Confirmed**; the lead cannot be closed as a duplicate without saying what survives it, so the disqualification report always links to something you can open.

> Anonymous Web-to-Lead submissions are always accepted. If the public form cannot check for existing records (it deliberately has no read access to your CRM data), the lead lands unflagged — review it from the normal lead queue.

### When the surviving record is later deleted

Deleting a Contact, or the Account above it, is never blocked by a lead that pointed at it — including a lead you already closed as a confirmed duplicate. What happens to the lead depends on how far the review had got:

- **Still Suspected** — nothing was decided, so the flag is dropped entirely. The lead stops claiming to duplicate anything and goes back to the normal queue.
- **Confirmed by a reviewer** — the verdict stands. Duplicate Status stays **Confirmed** and **Duplicate Of** changes to **Erased Record**, so the lead reads as *"a confirmed duplicate of a record that has since been erased"*. The disqualification and its reason are untouched.

**Erased Record** is not something you can choose. It is only ever written when the record a lead named is actually deleted, which is why seeing it on a lead is reliable evidence that the deletion happened rather than that someone typed it. This is what lets an erasure request be carried out in full without quietly deleting a reviewer's finding along with it.

## Converting a qualified lead

When a lead is qualified, click **Convert**. A small form pops up asking:

- Should we also create an Opportunity?
- If yes — what's the opportunity name and amount?

The system then, in a single step:

1. Creates an **Account** from the lead's company information — or reuses the one you already have. The match ignores capitalisation and extra spaces, so a lead for `ACME  Corp` lands on your existing `Acme Corp` instead of creating a second account. It is not a fuzzy match: `Acme Corp` and `Acme Corporation` are still two different companies.
2. Creates a **Contact** from the lead's person information, attached to that account, marked as primary.
3. *(Optional)* Creates an **Opportunity** in the *Prospecting* stage with a 90-day close date.
4. Marks the lead as **Converted** and locks it.
5. Sends you a confirmation email summarising what was created.

After conversion, the lead becomes read-only — all future activity happens on the new account, contact and opportunity.

## How the AI Copilot helps

When you open a lead, the AI assistant is available in the side panel. Try asking:

- *"Qualify this lead"* — runs BANT, gives you a score and a one-line justification per dimension.
- *"Is this a hot lead?"* — quick yes/no plus reasoning.
- *"Draft an intro email"* — writes a personalised first outreach.

The Copilot will recommend the next action (e.g., schedule a call, send a follow-up, disqualify).

## Standard list views

The Leads list curates no tab row, so the switcher offers every saved view there is — eight of them, in this order:

| View | What it shows |
| --- | --- |
| **All Leads** *(the landing view)* | Every lead, newest first, with the name as the link into the record plus company, email, status, score, source and owner. 25 rows a page, export to CSV or XLSX, and **Add to Campaign** on the selection toolbar. On an empty org it says so — *No Leads Yet · Get started by creating your first lead*. |
| **My Leads** | The leads you own, highest star rating first and newest within a rating. Also reachable from **My Work › My Leads** in the sidebar. |
| **🔥 Hot Leads** | Leads still **New** or **Contacted** rated **4 stars or better** — exactly the leads new-lead routing calls hot, gives a next-day follow-up date and alerts their owner about ("assign within 24h"). Ordered by **Next Follow-up Date**, soonest first, with phone, email and owner on the row so it can be worked top to bottom. |
| **High Priority** | The same population — **4 stars and up**, still New or Contacted — laid out to scan rather than to work through: no follow-up date, but the lead source on the row and rows tinted by rating (green at 5, orange at 4). |
| **Suspected Duplicates** | The review queue described above: leads whose **Duplicate Status** is *Suspected*, oldest suspicion first, with the duplicate-link fields on the row. Working it drains it — a lead set to *Confirmed* or cleared drops out. When there is nothing to review it says *No Suspected Duplicates · Nothing to review — every re-captured email has been checked.* |
| **Lead Pipeline** | The kanban board, one column per status, each column summing **Annual Revenue**. Opening a card slides the lead in from the side rather than taking you off the board. |
| **Lead Calendar** | Leads placed on a month calendar by the day they arrived, titled by company and coloured by status — the shape of your inflow. |
| **Lead Cards** | Gallery cards, one per lead, titled by company with name, email, phone, status and rating on the face. |

## Tips for sales reps

- ✅ Keep the **Next Follow-up Date** honest — it is what **🔥 Hot Leads** sorts on, so a stale date buries a live lead at the bottom of the queue. A new lead gets one automatically (four stars or better is due tomorrow, everything else in three days), and **Schedule Follow-up** re-stamps it with the due date of the task you just filed.
- ✅ Set the stars yourself when you have a strong gut feel — a rating you supply on create is kept as-is, and the automatic one only fills in when you leave it blank.
- ✅ Don't convert too early — a converted lead can't be reverted. Use *Qualified* status while you confirm.
- ❌ Don't keep noise in *New* forever — disqualify after 3 failed contact attempts.

## Tips for admins

- The score formula lives in the lead's automation. To re-weight it, edit `src/objects/lead.hook.ts` (binds to `crm_lead`). See [Customization](/docs/customization/extending-objects).
- Web-to-Lead can be enabled by granting the Guest Portal profile *insert-only* on the lead object — see [Administration › Profiles](/docs/administration/profiles).
- To add a new industry or lead source, edit the lead object's picklists — see [Customization](/docs/customization/extending-objects).
