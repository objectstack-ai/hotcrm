# Dashboards

HotCRM ships with five dashboards. Each one is built for a specific role and answers the questions that role asks every day.

Every tile listed on this page is a tile the app actually ships. The lists are checked against the dashboard metadata in CI (`test/docs-dashboard-tiles.test.ts`), so a tile that is renamed, added or removed in the product makes this page fail the build rather than quietly start lying.

## The five dashboards

| Dashboard | Built for | Answers |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 🏠 **CRM Overview** | Everyone who reads opportunities | "What's going on across the business today?" |
| 📈 **Sales Performance** | Sales reps, managers | "How healthy is my pipeline, and are we winning?" |
| ☎️ **Sales Activity** | Sales managers, account teams | "Who is talking to customers, and which accounts have gone quiet?" |
| 🎧 **Customer Service** | Service agents, managers | "How are we doing on cases and SLAs?" |
| 👔 **Executive Overview** | Execs, finance | "Are we hitting the number?" |

All five respect [sharing rules](/docs/administration/sharing-and-security) — what you see depends on your role. The three built on opportunities — CRM Overview, Sales Performance and Executive Overview — are left out of the sidebar for anyone who cannot read opportunities, and for a service agent or service manager by position.

**A hidden dashboard is not a locked one.** Executive Overview is also left out of the sidebar for anyone holding the `sales_rep`, `service_agent` or `service_manager` position, so their app opens on a page built for their day — **Sales Home** for a rep — instead of the company-level view. That is a shorter menu for focus, not an access restriction: a rep who opens the dashboard's link still sees it. What any dashboard shows is decided by record visibility, which [sharing rules](/docs/administration/sharing-and-security#how-the-layers-combine) set: every number adds up only the records the viewer may read, so a rep sees the executive layout over their own share of the data, never a deal they could not open themselves.

### A trend arrow is a real comparison

A KPI tile shows a "vs previous period" or "vs previous year" delta only when it declares a comparison, and the console then runs a second query over the earlier window. On CRM Overview that is Total Revenue, Deals Won and Avg Deal Size; on Sales Performance, Open Pipeline, Closed Won and Avg Deal Size; on Customer Service, Open Cases, Avg Resolution Time and Open Cases Past SLA; on Executive Overview, Total Revenue (YTD). A *(Selected Period)* tile compares with the window of the same length just before the picker's, so with the picker at All time there is no earlier window and the tile shows its bare number. No percentage is typed by hand: every one changes with the data.

## 🏠 CRM Overview

The cross-functional home dashboard — revenue, pipeline and deal mix on one page.

**Tiles**:

- **Total Revenue (Selected Period)** — closed-won revenue in the selected period.
- **Open Deals (Selected Period)** — open opportunities whose close date falls in the selected period.
- **Deals Won (Selected Period)** — closed-won deals in the selected period.
- **Avg Deal Size (Selected Period)** — average value of the deals closed won in the selected period.
- **Pipeline by Stage** — open opportunity value at each sales stage (horizontal bar chart).
- **Lead Source** — pipeline value by acquisition channel (donut).
- **List Price by Category** — catalog list prices summed per product category. It reads the price book, not sales: no tile on this page sums sold line items (bar).
- **Pipeline by Owner** — open pipeline value, deal count and average deal size per rep (table).

**Controls**: a date-range picker over the opportunity close date (defaults to this quarter) and an Owner filter.

Great as a daily start-of-day landing page.

## 📈 Sales Performance

The sales team's daily operational dashboard.

**Tiles**:

- **Open Pipeline (Selected Period)** — value of the open opportunities whose close date falls in the selected period.
- **Closed Won (QTD)** — revenue closed this quarter.
- **Open Deals (Selected Period)** — the count of those same open opportunities.
- **Avg Deal Size (QTD)** — average value of the deals closed won this quarter.
- **Win Rate (12M)** — deals won as a share of deals *settled* (won + lost) in the last 12 months. Open pipeline is not in the denominator.
- **Deals Won (12M)** — the numerator behind that percentage.
- **Deals Lost (12M)** — the other half of its denominator, so both numbers are always on screen next to the rate.
- **Pipeline by Stage** — open opportunity value at each sales stage (horizontal bar chart).
- **Pipeline by Forecast Category** — open pipeline grouped by forecast category.
- **Lead Source** — where the pipeline is coming from (donut).
- **Open Pipeline by Owner** — in-flight value, deal count and average win probability per rep (table).
- **Quota Attainment by Rep** — current-quarter quota, closed revenue and attainment per rep, read from the forecast snapshots. It stays empty until the nightly forecast snapshot has opened the current quarter and a manager has set each rep's quota; the demo data does not open one for you.
- **Win / Loss by Rep** — won, lost, settled, win rate and won revenue per rep (table).
- **Win / Loss by Lead Source** — the same breakdown by acquisition channel: which sources produce deals that actually close.
- **Why We Lose** — lost deals by loss reason. Every closed-lost deal carries a reason (it is [required at close](/docs/sales/opportunities)), so this chart has no "unattributed" slice — an empty chart means no losses in the window, not an unfilled field.
- **Pipeline by Stage × Lead Source** — cross-tab of open opportunity amount by stage and source (pivot).

**Controls**: a date-range picker over the close date (defaults to this quarter), plus Sales Rep and Deal Type filters. Quota Attainment by Rep is pinned to the current quarter and ignores both, because a quota is defined for a period and re-slicing it by an arbitrary range would compare a full target against partial revenue.

Role-aware: reps see their own records, managers see their team, per the sharing rules.

## ☎️ Sales Activity

Coverage and customer-health view: who is talking to customers, how often, and where the silence is.

**Tiles**:

- **Interactions Logged** — calls and meetings that actually happened.
- **Meetings Booked** — meetings on the calendar that have not happened yet.
- **Customer Minutes** — total time spent in front of customers.
- **Tasks Completed** — follow-ups closed out, the other half of activity.
- **Activity by Rep** — logged interactions per owner (bar).
- **Activity Volume by Week** — interactions per week: is the team speeding up or going quiet?
- **Activity Mix** — calls vs meetings vs demos (donut).
- **Where the Activity Lands** — which part of the funnel is getting the attention.
- **Interactions on Deals** — logged interactions linked to an opportunity.
- **Open Deals** — opportunities still in play, to read the previous tile against.
- **Quiet 30+ Days** — active accounts with no logged interaction in a month.
- **Quiet 60+ Days** — two months of silence, the at-risk threshold.
- **Quiet 90+ Days** — a quarter with no contact: intervene or write it off.

**Controls**: a Rep filter. There is deliberately **no** date-range picker: time is carried on an axis instead — Activity Volume by Week buckets by week, and the three quiet-account tiles window their own 30 / 60 / 90-day thresholds.

## 🎧 Customer Service

The service team's daily ops view.

**Tiles**:

- **Open Cases** — cases that are not yet closed.
- **Critical Cases** — open cases at critical priority.
- **Avg Resolution Time** — mean time to close, in hours.
- **Open Cases Past SLA** — open cases whose SLA deadline has passed. Its base is open cases only: a case closed late leaves this count when it closes.
- **Cases by Status** — workload distribution across the pipeline (donut).
- **Cases by Priority** — open case mix by urgency (pie).
- **Cases by Origin** — Email / Phone / Web / Chat / Portal (bar).
- **Daily Case Volume** — new cases created over the last 30 days.
- **SLA Met (Closed Cases)** — share of closed cases that met their SLA, against a 95% target line (gauge). Its base is closed cases only: an open case is in neither the numerator nor the denominator.
- **My Open Cases by Priority** — the signed-in user's own open cases and their SLA-violation rate per priority (table). It ignores the Agent filter: it always shows your own cases.

**Controls**: a date-range picker over the date a case was created (defaults to the last 90 days), Agent and Priority filters, and a one-minute refresh — service desks need fresh numbers. It is the only picker that windows a case date rather than the opportunity close date, and the only one that does not default to this quarter, so every number on this page is bounded by the last 90 days until you widen it. Daily Case Volume opts out of the picker and keeps its own 30-day window, so the chart under that title always covers the 30 days it names.

## 👔 Executive Overview

The exec / finance view of the business.

**Tiles**:

- **Total Revenue (YTD)** — closed-won revenue this year.
- **Active Accounts** — customers with at least one active relationship.
- **Total Contacts** — people in the address book.
- **Open Leads** — unconverted leads in the funnel.
- **Revenue Trend** — closed-won revenue over the last 12 months. This is the only dashboard that carries the trend, so it has one home.
- **Revenue by Industry** — YTD closed-won revenue split by customer industry (donut).
- **Pipeline by Stage** — open opportunity value at each sales stage (horizontal bar chart).
- **New Accounts** — account creation cadence over the last 6 months.
- **Accounts by Industry** — total annual revenue and account count per industry (table).

**Controls**: a date-range picker over the close date (defaults to this quarter), plus Owner and Lead Source filters. The four KPI tiles state their own period (YTD, "active", "unconverted") and are not narrowed by the picker.

This dashboard is the basis for the weekly leadership review.

## What you can change

- **Date range** — four of the five dashboards carry a picker, and all four accept a custom range. CRM Overview, Sales Performance and Executive Overview window the opportunity close date and default to this quarter; Customer Service windows the date a case was created and defaults to the last 90 days. Sales Activity is the only dashboard with no picker, for the reason given in its section above. Every window is resolved on the server, on UTC calendar days: "last 7 days" means the seven UTC calendar days ending with today's UTC date, both ends inclusive. Your own clock does not enter into it — a reader in UTC+8 opening a dashboard at 03:00 local time is looking at a window that ended at the previous UTC midnight, not at their own.
- **Filters** — each dashboard declares its own: Owner everywhere, plus Deal Type on Sales Performance, Priority on Customer Service, and Lead Source on Executive Overview.
- **Refresh** — Customer Service refreshes every minute, Sales Performance every three, the other three every five.

A dashboard filter is matched against the **object** behind each widget's dataset, not against that dataset's declared dimensions, and it is ANDed into the query of every widget bound to it. That is why Customer Service can offer an Agent filter even though `case_metrics` declares no owner dimension: `owner_id` is a field on the case, and the filter needs nothing more than that. Filtering is all it buys — a widget still cannot **group by** a field the dataset does not declare, which is how [SLA & Escalation](/docs/service/sla-and-escalation) can say, correctly, that no report and no dashboard widget breaks cases down by agent, on the same app whose service desk filters by one. It cuts the other way too: a filter naming a field a widget's own object lacks would ask that widget's query for a column that does not exist, so a widget that cannot answer a filter opts out of it by name (`filterBindings`) instead of quietly ignoring it.

Some tiles opt out of the dashboard's own filters on purpose. Revenue Trend, List Price by Category and Quota Attainment by Rep keep the window they are labelled with, so a narrower date picker cannot silently redefine the number under its own title.

## Where the numbers come from

Every tile is bound to a dataset — the semantic layer that defines what "pipeline", "settled deal" or "SLA violation" means, once, for every tile and report that uses it. See [Datasets and cubes](/docs/analytics/cubes) for the measures and dimensions available, and [Reports](/docs/analytics/reports) for the record-level views behind the aggregates.

Definitions that appear on more than one dashboard are shared rather than re-typed: Pipeline by Stage and Avg Deal Size are one definition each, used by three and two dashboards respectively, so "open pipeline" cannot come to mean different things on different pages.

The window is not shared, and each KPI title says which one it reads. *(Selected Period)* follows the dashboard's date picker, so it shows this quarter until you pick another range; *(QTD)*, *(YTD)* and *(12M)* are fixed windows the picker cannot move; and the KPI tiles on **Sales Home** say *(All Time)* because they read every record. The same measure over the same window has the same title on every page, so two different numbers always sit under two different titles.

## Tips

- ✅ Before a pipeline review, read **Win / Loss by Rep** next to **Why We Lose** — the first says who is converting, the second says what is beating you.
- ✅ Work the **Quiet 90+ Days** tile weekly. An account with no logged interaction in a quarter is either dormant or already gone, and the tile is only as good as the interactions your team logs.
- ✅ On the service desk, read the two SLA tiles as two different populations: **Open Cases Past SLA** counts open cases that are already late (work to do now), while **SLA Met (Closed Cases)** is the rate over cases already closed (how the desk did). A late case moves from the first to the second when it closes.
- ✅ Read **Interactions on Deals** against **Open Deals**: the ratio, not either number alone, is the coverage question.
