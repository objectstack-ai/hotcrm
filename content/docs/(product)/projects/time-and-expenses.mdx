# Time and Expenses

## Entering your week

Open **My Week** under **Time and Expenses**. It lists your time entries of this week, Monday to Sunday, grouped by project, and you edit them in place. To add a day's work, choose **New** and fill in the **Project**, the **Date**, the **Hours**, and the **Task** if you like. Leave the **Timesheet** empty:

- Your timesheet on that project whose period holds the date is used.
- When there is none, a draft timesheet is created for you for that week, Monday to Sunday, and the entry goes into it.

So a week across three projects is entered line by line and makes three timesheets, one per project, each approved by its own project manager. Change an entry's **Project** or **Date** and it moves to the matching timesheet. An entry cannot leave, or join, a timesheet that is already submitted.

**My Time** lists all your entries, grouped by timesheet, newest first: use it to fill in last week.

When the week is done, open **Submit This Week**. It lists your timesheets that touch this week, one per project. Select them all and choose **Submit**: each is submitted on its own and goes to its project's manager. If one project is locked or closed, only that timesheet is refused and the others are submitted. A single timesheet also has a **Submit** button.

## Timesheets

A **Timesheet** holds one person's hours on one project for one period, usually a week. Open **My Timesheets** and choose **New**:

- Pick the **Project**, and the **Period Start** and **Period End**. A period covers at most 31 days.
- Leave **Timesheet** (the name) empty and it fills itself from the project and the period, and follows them if you change either later. A name you type is kept.
- The **Approver** is the project's Project Manager at that moment, and cannot be changed.

You keep one timesheet per project per period: a second one for the same project and the same period start is refused, naming the first, so add the hours there.

### Time entries

Each day's work is a **Time Entry** in the timesheet's **Time Entries** tab: the **Date**, the **Hours** (from a quarter of an hour up to 24), whether they are **Billable**, and a note.

- The date must fall inside the timesheet's period.
- You can only book hours on a project you are staffed on. Otherwise the entry is refused, naming the project: ask its project manager to add you.
- The entry copies the **Cost Rate** and **Bill Rate** from your staffing's rate card when it is saved, converted to an hourly rate (a day is 8 hours), and works out its **Cost** and **Amount to Bill**. A non-billable entry, or a member staffed as not billable, bills nothing.

The timesheet's **Totals** add up its entries: **Total Hours**, **Total Cost** and **Amount to Bill**.

### Submitting and approving

| Status | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| **Draft** | You are still filling it in |
| **Submitted** | Sent to the approver. Its hours are frozen |
| **Approved** | The hours count in the project's cost. Final |
| **Rejected** | Sent back. Move it to Draft, correct it and submit again |

When the week is done, choose **Submit** (or submit the whole week from **Submit This Week**). A submitted timesheet's entries can no longer be added, changed or removed, and the timesheet itself is locked while it waits.

The approver decides in their **Inbox** (under **My Work**), with **Approve** or **Reject**. **Timesheets to Approve** lists the same timesheets, to open and read before deciding.

- **Approved**: its hours count in the project's cost.
- **Rejected**: move it back to **Draft**, correct it and submit it again, which asks the approver again.
- **Changed your mind?** Recall it from your Inbox while it waits. It goes back to **Draft**.

Nobody sets **Approved** or **Rejected** by hand: those are written by the approval. Only an administrator can, to repair a timesheet whose approval cannot run.

**Approval needs approvals on the deployment.** The approval that asks the approver, and the **Inbox** they decide in, come with the deployment's approvals. On a deployment that does not include them, nobody is asked: a timesheet you submit saves, reads **Submitted** and stays there, and its cost never reaches the project on its own. Its approver cannot decide it either: setting **Approved** or **Rejected** is refused with a message that sends them to an Inbox the deployment does not have. There an administrator decides every submitted timesheet, by setting its **Status** to **Approved** or **Rejected**; an approved one then counts in the project's cost as usual.

> **Nobody approves their own hours.** When the approver submits their own timesheet, they are not asked: it goes to an administrator instead.

**A one-person organization.** Nobody approves a request they submitted, an administrator included, so a timesheet needs a second user who can approve it: the project's Project Manager, or an administrator. A timesheet keeps the approver it was created with, so one created while you were its project's Project Manager goes to an administrator other than you. Until that person has been added, a submitted timesheet waits in **Submitted** and nobody can decide it: recall it from your Inbox, and submit it again once they have been added.

## Expenses

Expenses are their own module, for everyone: recording one, its fields and who approves it are on the [Expenses](/docs/expenses) page. Here is what a project adds.

An expense can name a **Project**, and project staff find **My Expenses** and **Reports to Approve** here, under **Time and Expenses**. **Submit for Reimbursement** puts each project's expenses on an expense report of their own, which names the project. That report is approved by the project's Project Manager, who is named as its **Approver** when it is submitted, instead of the spender's own manager; the finance step from a total of 2,000 is the same as for any report ([who approves it](/docs/expenses#who-approves-it)). Each expense is approved when its report is.

On a deployment that does not include approvals, a submitted expense report waits in **Submitted** the same way, with nobody asked. An administrator decides it by setting the report's **Status** to **Approved** or **Rejected**; its expenses follow the report, and approved ones count in the project's cost.

A [travel request](/docs/expenses/travel-requests) can name a **Project** too, and project staff find **My Travel Requests** here, beside **My Expenses**. A request on a project is approved by the project's Project Manager, in one step whatever the amount, so the trip and its expenses go to the same person; one with no project goes to the traveler's own manager. The project page lists the project's travel requests under **Related**. A request is a plan, not a cost: it adds nothing to the project's cost, and the lock below does not stop it.

## How they become the project's cost

Only **Approved** timesheets and expenses count:

- A timesheet's **Total Cost** adds to the project's **Labor Cost**, and its **Amount to Bill** to **Approved to Bill**.
- An expense's **Amount** adds to the project's **Expense Cost**, and to **Approved to Bill** when it is billable.

Draft, submitted and rejected ones count nowhere. The project's [cost and margin](/docs/projects#money) move the moment one is approved.

## When the project is locked

A project manager or finance can tick **Lock Time and Expenses** on a project, usually after a [budget notice](/docs/projects/projects-and-staffing#budget-lock). While it is ticked, submitting a timesheet on that project is refused, naming the project. Submitting an expense report on it is refused too, with the reason, and the report stays in **Draft**. **Submit for Reimbursement** and **Submit for a Trip** still create that project's report, leave it in Draft with its expenses on it, send the reports of the other projects, and end with an error naming the report that was not sent. Keep them as drafts: once the lock is lifted, submit them as usual.

## When the project is over

A **Closed** or **Cancelled** project takes no more time or spend. Creating a timesheet on it is refused, and so is moving one onto it; the message names the project and its status. A draft timesheet already on the project stays where it is and can still be edited, but submitting it is refused: move it to an open project to submit it there.

An expense's **Project** picker does not offer a Closed or Cancelled project. An expense report already on one (for expenses written before the project closed, say) cannot be submitted: the submission is refused with the reason, and the report stays in **Draft**. Delete the report, move its expenses to an open project and submit them again.

A **Completed** project still takes late timesheets and expenses. To stop them, tick **Lock Time and Expenses**, or close the project.
